About This Project
A Localized Framework for Gulf Conflict Management

This dataset codes Gulf Arab state mediation at the episode level, applying the Bercovitch-Gartner (2006) contingency framework alongside Gulf-specific variables developed to capture features of non-Western, interested-state mediation that mainstream frameworks do not address.

The central argument: Gulf states mediate differently from each other because each state has built different structural conditions of access to conflict parties - hosting infrastructure, financial leverage, diplomatic neutrality, third-party mandates - and those conditions determine which tools it can credibly deploy and what outcomes it can achieve. The paper operationalises this through two Gulf-specific variables absent from existing frameworks: access mechanism (the verifiable structural condition enabling each episode) and recognition management (how Gulf states engage non-state actors they do not formally recognise).

Empirical Analysis - Pre-Publication

Key Findings from the Dataset

Ten empirically grounded findings drawn from 94 coded Gulf mediation episodes. Each finding tests or extends Bercovitch-Gartner (2006) hypotheses for non-Western, interested-state mediators.

01

Gulf Directive Mediation Does Not Produce What B-G Predicts

76% of Directive episodes produce Access-Transactional outcomes

Bercovitch-Gartner predict Directive strategies produce ceasefires and full settlements in high-intensity conflicts. In the Gulf data, 22 of 29 Directive episodes (76%) produce Access-Transactional outcomes instead. All six UAE Russia-Ukraine POW exchanges (2022-2024) are coded Directive yet produce Access-Transactional outcomes. Qatar's November 2023 Gaza mediation - Directive shuttle diplomacy between Israel and Hamas - produced a humanitarian pause and 80 hostage releases, not a ceasefire. Oman's Iran-UK prisoner release (2022) follows the same pattern. The only Directive episodes that produce ceasefires are those where the mediation is sustained across multiple sequential episodes in the same arena (Qatar-Gaza 2012, 2021, January 2025). H2A and H2B are disconfirmed for Gulf mediators.

Implication: Gulf states lack the enforcement capacity that makes Directive mediation coercive in Western/UN contexts. Their leverage is relational and transactional. The same strategy label covers fundamentally different behaviours.
02

Procedural Hosting Produces Formal Agreements in Lower-Intensity Conflicts

9 of 12 Full Settlements come from Procedural episodes

H2D is confirmed: Procedural strategies produce Full and Partial Settlements predominantly in Low and Medium intensity conflicts. Paradigm cases: the Doha Agreement (Lebanon 2008) ended a High-intensity intra-state armed standoff through Qatar Hosting/Formulation. The Taif Agreement (Lebanon 1989) ended a civil war through Saudi Hosting/Formulation. The Doha Document for Peace in Darfur (2011) produced a Partial Settlement through Qatar Hosting/Formulation in High-intensity civil war. The UAE-led Ethiopia-Eritrea Jeddah Agreement (2018) - a Full Settlement in a Medium-intensity interstate war - is also Procedural. Procedural works when both parties want the legitimating authority of a Gulf host and neither party is a non-state actor that the host cannot formally convene. The 12 Full Settlements in the dataset split across strategies as follows: 9 from Procedural, 3 from Facilitate Communications in low-intensity contexts, and 0 from Directive. Procedural is the settlement-producing strategy in this dataset.

Implication: Hosting infrastructure and formal convening capacity are prerequisites for Procedural success. A Gulf state without an established venue and institutional standing cannot replicate these outcomes through strategy choice alone.

Bypass produces Access-Transactional outcomes in 11 of 17 episodes (65%) and never produces Full Settlement. Paradigm cases: Oman-Houthis detainee channels (2015, 2016, 2022, 2025) - all Bypass, all Access-Transactional. Buffer produces Ceasefire and Access-Transactional only: Qatar-Hamas via Palestinian Authority and US framing across multiple Gaza episodes (2012, 2021, 2023, 2024, January 2025). Bracket is the only mode that produces Full Settlement: Qatar-Taliban culminating in the 2020 Doha Agreement after seven years of Bracket management. Normalise produces the highest rate of Partial or Full Settlement: Saudi Taif 1989, Qatar Doha Agreement 2008, Qatar-Sudan-Eritrea 1999. The recognition management variable, absent from B-G, predicts outcome type in NSA conflicts more cleanly than strategy. HG3 confirmed directionally.

Implication: Before asking what strategy to use, ask how the mediator manages the recognition question. A Gulf state in Bypass mode cannot produce formal agreements by upgrading its strategy - it would need to change its recognition posture first, which is a structural not tactical decision.
04

The Detainee Channel Is the Most Outcome-Consistent Tool in the Dataset

100% of detainee channel episodes (N=17) produce Access-Transactional

No other mediation tool produces a single outcome category at 100%. Hosting/Formulation produces the highest rate of Full Settlement. The tool deployed predicts the outcome more cleanly than the B-G strategy category. HG2 confirmed: Gulf mediation is tool-based diplomacy. The instrument chosen tells you what the outcome will be before talks begin.

Implication: Understanding Gulf mediation requires tool-level analysis, not just strategy-level categorisation.

H3 confirmed for same-actor sequences. When a different Gulf state previously succeeded in the same conflict, outcomes are significantly weaker. Relational capital is state-specific and non-transferable. HG4 confirmed. The Yemen sequence (Qatar 2008, Saudi 2011, Oman 2015-2025, Saudi 2023) illustrates the gap precisely.

Implication: Gulf mediation handoffs do not work without deliberate coordination. Substitution is not the same as succession.
06

Humanitarian and Political Tracks Are Parallel Systems

63% of all episodes do not sequence toward political settlement

Of 94 episodes, 59 (63%) have sequencing_to_political coded No. Humanitarian access objectives produce Access-Transactional outcomes across all conflict intensity levels. The distribution is flat with respect to intensity, meaning humanitarian tools are not stepping stones. HG5 confirmed: Gulf humanitarian mediation is a self-contained mode, not a gateway to political resolution.

Workshop implication: Expecting Gulf humanitarian mediation to evolve naturally into political settlement is a category error. The HDP Nexus requires deliberate design, not organic sequencing.
07

Gulf States Operate Across the Full Conflict Intensity Spectrum

73% of episodes involve high-intensity conflicts

B-G predict regional actors concentrate in Low-intensity conflicts, leaving High-intensity cases to major powers and international organisations. Gulf states disconfirm this: 67 of 94 episodes (71%) are High-intensity. Qatar mediates between the US and the Taliban (High-intensity civil war) and between Israel and Hamas across five High-intensity asymmetric episodes. Oman holds the US-Iran channel across four High-intensity interstate diplomatic episodes spanning 2002 to 2025. The UAE facilitates six High-intensity Russia-Ukraine POW exchanges and the High-intensity Ethiopia-Eritrea settlement. Saudi Arabia mediates the High-intensity Jeddah process for Sudan and the High-intensity Yemen civil war across multiple episodes. B-G's hierarchy was built on Cold War and immediate post-Cold War data when the US, UN, and European powers genuinely dominated high-intensity mediation. Gulf states entered the high-intensity space not by becoming major powers but by holding relational assets - hosting infrastructure, financial leverage, diplomatic neutrality - that major powers lacked or had sacrificed. The B-G intensity-mediator hierarchy requires revision when relational access substitutes for coercive leverage.

Implication: Mediator effectiveness at high intensity is not a function of size, resources, or institutional mandate. It is a function of the specific access conditions the mediator holds with the conflict parties.

Qatar uses the widest tool repertoire, explaining its structural indispensability. Oman uses Backchannel and Detainee channel almost exclusively. Saudi Arabia concentrates 67% of episodes in Hosting/Formulation. UAE concentrates in Quiet facilitation and Detainee channel. These patterns are stable across decades, not case-by-case choices.

Implication: The four mediation postures emerge from the data, not from assertion. Gulf states cannot simply choose different tools.
09

Access Mechanism Determines Strategy Selection

Diplomatic neutrality (Oman, N=18) predicts Facilitate Communications in 100% of episodes. Financial leverage (UAE) predicts Directive and quiet facilitation at highest rates. Arab League framework and security partnership (Saudi Arabia) predict Procedural strategy at majority rate. Hosting history and third-party mandate (Qatar) produce a hybrid Directive/Procedural profile reflecting Qatar's broader access portfolio. Strategy is not freely chosen: it is downstream of the structural access conditions each state brings to a conflict, not of strategic preference.

HG1 confirmed: Access mechanism predicts strategy selection. A Gulf state cannot credibly deploy a tool its access conditions do not support. This is the foundational claim of the paper.

Mediation Posture
Qatar
The Engaged Guarantor

Qatar is the only Gulf state that deploys all four recognition management modes, with Normalise as its most common mode (N=14), followed by Buffer (N=10), Bracket (N=9), and Bypass (N=5). This distribution makes Qatar structurally indispensable across the full spectrum of NSA conflicts: it can pursue formal agreements, hold recognition in abeyance, or facilitate exchanges without any legitimacy framework depending on what each episode requires. Its access conditions combine hosting history (Hamas political bureau, Taliban office) with explicit third-party mandates from the US that gives it unique leverage as a bridge between adversaries and Washington.

Mediation Posture
Oman
The Invisible Interlocutor

Oman operates on documented diplomatic neutrality as its core access condition, deliberately maintaining working relationships with all sides of conflicts that larger Gulf states cannot approach. Its access condition of documented diplomatic neutrality - a deliberate foreign policy posture sustained over decades - gives it entry to adversaries that other Gulf states cannot approach without political cost. The backchannel specialisation that results generates high rates of transactional and access outcomes. Notably, 89% of Oman's episodes are high-intensity, disconfirming B-G's prediction that small-state mediators concentrate in lower-intensity conflicts. Oman operates at high intensity precisely because its neutrality gives it access that other mediators lose when tension escalates.

Mediation Posture
Saudi Arabia
The Hierarchical Host

Saudi Arabia mediates through formal hosting processes, using Arab League institutional standing and financial leverage to convene state-level parties at Riyadh or Jeddah. What the data reveals is that Saudi recognition management is exclusively Normalise or Bracket: it never uses Bypass or Buffer modes. This structural characteristic explains both Saudi Arabia's strength in state-level political conflicts (Taif 1989, Mecca 2007, Jeddah 2018, Al-Ula 2021) and its structural limitation in conflicts requiring direct engagement with non-state actors that it refuses to recognise.

Mediation Posture
United Arab Emirates
The Results Broker

The UAE deploys mediation as a results-oriented, commercially intelligent tool. Financial leverage is its primary access condition, enabling discrete and visible transactions in contexts where political commitment would be too costly. The Russia-Ukraine POW exchange series is the paradigm: six separate exchanges across 2022-2024, each Grade A sourced, each producing a concrete humanitarian outcome, none sequencing toward political settlement. UAE has the lowest sequencing rate of any state in the dataset (1 of 17 episodes = 6%).

Pivot Table Results - Pre-Publication

Empirical Analysis

Visual breakdown of all ten pivot tables. Click any finding panel to expand. All results traceable to the 94-case dataset.

PT1

B-G Strategy + Outcome

Does Gulf Directive mediation produce what Bercovitch-Gartner predict?

KEY FINDING: 76% of Directive episodes (22/29) produce Access-Transactional outcomes, not ceasefires or full settlements as B-G (2006) predict. Procedural strategies account for all 9 Full Settlements. H2A and H2B disconfirmed for Gulf mediators.
FOR THE PAPER: Foundational table showing B-G framework needs localising. Gulf Directive mediation is transactional - it works when both parties want a discrete exchange, not because the mediator applies coercive pressure.
PT2

Strategy + Outcome by Conflict Intensity

Does intensity change Gulf strategy effectiveness?

KEY FINDING: In high-intensity conflicts, Directive strategies still produce Access-Transactional not Ceasefire/Full Settlement (17/21 = 81%). H2D confirmed: Procedural works in lower-intensity. B-G intensity-mediator hierarchy does not hold for Gulf cases.
FOR THE PAPER: Gulf states operate across intensity spectrum using strategies B-G did not predict. High-intensity conflict management via relational access rather than coercive leverage is the Gulf-specific mechanism.
PT3

Recognition Management + Outcome (NSA Subset)

The paper's most original table. N=37 episodes with direct NSA engagement.

KEY FINDING: Bypass (N=18) clusters in Access-Transactional (50%). Buffer (N=5) produces Ceasefire and Access-Transactional only. Bracket (N=14) is the only mode showing Full Settlement. Normalise (N=10) produces Full/Partial Settlement at highest rate. Recognition mode predicts outcome type more cleanly than strategy. HG3 confirmed.
FOR THE PAPER: This variable does not exist in B-G or Pruitt. How a Gulf state manages the legitimacy problem with a non-state actor determines what outcomes are achievable before strategy even enters the picture.
PT4

Mediation Tool + Outcome

Does tool selection predict outcomes better than strategy alone?

KEY FINDING: Detainee channel: 17/17 episodes produce Access-Transactional (100%). Hosting/Formulation produces 9 Full Settlements. Backchannel is the most versatile tool. Tool selection is a stronger predictor of outcome than B-G strategy alone. HG1 confirmed.
FOR THE PAPER: Gulf mediation is tool-based. The same B-G strategy category (Directive) contains detainee channels (100% transactional) and shuttle mediation (mixed) - strategy label obscures the analytically important variation.
PT5 / PT5.2

Previous Mediation - Same vs Different Actor

Does prior success help? And does it transfer between Gulf states?

KEY FINDING: Prior Access-Transactional by same actor repeats in 52% of subsequent episodes. Prior Full Settlement by same actor: 100% carry-forward (N=1, Qatar-Afghanistan). Prior Full Settlement by DIFFERENT Gulf state: mixed outcomes - no reliable transfer. H3 confirmed for same-actor; HG4 confirmed - relational capital is state-specific.
FOR THE PAPER: Mediation capacity is embedded in specific relational relationships, not just conflict history. Disrupting an established Gulf mediation channel creates a gap other actors cannot fill with the same relational capital.
PT6

Gulf State + Mediation Tool (The Posture Table)

Tool repertoire by state - the four postures visible in one table.

KEY FINDING: Oman uses Backchannel (11) and Detainee channel (4) almost exclusively - narrowest and most specialised repertoire. Qatar distributes across all tools. Saudi Arabia concentrates in Hosting/Formulation (14/21 = 67%). UAE concentrates in Quiet facilitation (10) and Detainee channel (6). Tool repertoire is state-specific and stable.
FOR THE PAPER: Table visually confirms the four mediation postures. Labels emerge from the data, not from assertion. Gulf states are not interchangeable diplomatic resources.
PT7

Access Mechanism + B-G Strategy

Does access mechanism predict strategy selection? Updated from relational_basis - attributional categories removed.

KEY FINDING: Diplomatic neutrality (Oman, N=18) predicts Facilitate Communications in 100% of cases. Financial leverage predicts Directive (UAE transactional pattern). Arab League framework and Security partnership predict Procedural (Saudi hosting pattern). Hosting history and Third-party mandate concentrate in Directive and Procedural (Qatar hybrid posture). HG2 confirmed.
FOR THE PAPER: Mechanism table. Strategy selection is not random - it is downstream of the structural access conditions each state holds. Updated from relational_basis to access_mechanism: attributional categories removed.
PT8

Humanitarian Access + Intensity (HDP Nexus Table)

Do humanitarian outcomes sequence into political settlement?

KEY FINDING: Humanitarian access episodes (Yes, N=53) produce Access-Transactional and Ceasefire at high rates across ALL intensity levels - the distribution is flat with respect to intensity. Only 3 humanitarian access episodes produce Full Settlement. HG5 confirmed: humanitarian and political tracks are parallel, not sequential.
FOR THE WORKSHOP: Core finding for this workshop. If policymakers expect Gulf humanitarian mediation to naturally evolve into political settlement, the data says it does not. Connecting the tracks requires deliberate design.
PT9

Conflict Type + Gulf State

Do Gulf states specialise in different conflict environments?

KEY FINDING: Oman concentrates in Civil war (9/18) and Interstate diplomatic conflict. Qatar distributes across Civil war, Asymmetric conflict, and Interstate diplomatic conflict - broadest range. Saudi Arabia concentrates in Civil war (9/21). UAE concentrates in Interstate war (8/21 = 38%) - highest proportion of any state in this category. Conflict-type specialisation maps onto posture.
FOR THE PAPER: Supports the posture argument from the conflict-type dimension. Gulf states engage selectively in conflict types that match their access conditions, not simply responding opportunistically.
PT10 / PT10.2

Sequencing to Political Settlement by Gulf State

Which states convert mediation into political progress?

KEY FINDING: Oman has most Yes entries (8) but these sequence into enabling conditions for other processes, not formal agreements. Qatar has 5 Yes entries including the only confirmed Full Settlement sequence (Afghanistan). Saudi Arabia has 7 Yes entries mostly Partial Settlement. UAE has 3 Yes. No category dominates (59/96 = 61%). Political sequencing is the exception.
FOR THE WORKSHOP: Converting Gulf humanitarian mediation into political progress is possible but requires knowing which state's sequencing logic you are working with and creating the specific conditions that enable each state's form of sequencing.
